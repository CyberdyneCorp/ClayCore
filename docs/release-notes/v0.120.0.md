# claycore v0.120.0

Covers minors 0.117.0 through 0.120.0. The previous tag is v0.116.0
(2026-09-16). None of the four minors was ever tagged on its own; this tag is
cut at whatever the version line had reached.

**Read this one if you GRAB a mesh, if you sculpt the ADAPTIVE surface, if you
MASK a voxel edit, if you use the voxel GRAB's falloff, or if you bind ZBrush
Standard to `CLAY_OP_RELIEF`.** Two of the changes below alter what a shipped
entry point returns to a caller who changes nothing and recompiles nothing, and
no version gate announces either of them. Three more add surface: the adaptive
mesh gains undo, whole strokes, and whole strokes recorded as one undo step.

---

## The one to read first: a mesh Grab now reaches the whole drag

**A mesh `grab` moves the surface as far as the cursor moved** (#624, no ABI
change). `clay_mesh_sculptor_apply_stroke`, `clay_dynamic_sculptor_apply_stroke`,
`clay_multires_sculptor_apply_stroke`, their `_preset` and `_recorded` siblings,
`brush::apply_to_mesh` / `_multires` / `_dynamic`, and pyclay's
`MeshSculptor.apply_stroke` / `.apply_preset`,
`DynamicSculptor.apply_stroke` / `.apply_preset` and the multires pair all
answer differently for `grab` and for `grab` alone. Every other verb is
bit-identical.

A grab used to RE-GATHER its region around the stroke's first sample on every
stamp. The surface leaves that point, so the falloff weights shrank as the
gesture went on and the pull fell short of the drag. It now gathers ONCE, at the
first stamp, and carries the items, their captured positions and their weights
for the whole gesture: each stamp writes `captured + weight * (p_k − p_0)`.

**EVERY PRESET CHANGES BY A DIFFERENT AMOUNT, so there is no single factor to
divide by.** The shortfall was not a constant discount — it depended on the
brush radius and it compounded with the drag. Measured on a unit
`cube_sphere(24)`, spacing 0.1, macOS arm64, Release, `cpu-only`:

| brush radius | drag | reached before | reaches now | a host feel tuned against the old number now travels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | 0.6 | **22.66%** | 100% | 4.41x further |
| 0.30 | 0.6 | **41.10%** | 100% | 2.43x further |
| 0.50 | 0.6 | **60.56%** | 100% | 1.65x further |
| 0.15 | 1.5 | **9.22%** | 100% | 10.8x further |
| 0.30 | 1.5 | **18.21%** | 100% | 5.49x further |

The same numbers on the adaptive surface to within half a point (41.34%, 18.06%
at radius 0.30), because the two representations are held to agreeing and were
agreeing at 41% each. The "reached before" column is the FIXED path, measured by
driving `brush::apply_to_mesh` on a build of `origin/main` — not by hand-spelling
the old rule against the new library. Shipped reach is 100.00–100.03% on all
eight fixtures on both paths, the two agreeing to **0.0 – 1.6e-4**.

**On a curve a grab moves the CHORD, not the path the cursor travelled.** A
quarter arc of length 0.6 has a net displacement of 0.5402 and the surface moves
by 0.5402. This is what carrying one piece of surface means, and a host
measuring the surface against the cursor's trail will read it as a 90%
shortfall (the fixed path reaches 100.00% of the chord and 90.22% of the path).
It is not one. Any host-side calibration must use `last_sample − first_sample`,
not the accumulated path length.

**A grab is now CHEAPER, not dearer.** One gather per gesture instead of one per
stamp. Re-measured on the SHIPPED code — both arms driven through this library,
the old rule hand-spelled against `MeshSculptor::stamp` / `DynamicSculptor::stamp`
with a per-stamp centre, interleaved, first repeat discarded, medians:

| fixture, `cube_sphere(24)`, spacing 0.1 | stamps | re-gathering | carried | |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| fixed, pull-out 0.6, r 0.30 | 11 | 0.1350 ms | 0.0692 ms | **1.95x** |
| fixed, pull-out 1.5, r 0.30 | 26 | 0.2784 ms | 0.1491 ms | **1.87x** |
| adaptive, pull-out 0.6, r 0.30 | 11 | 55.56 ms | 36.48 ms | **1.52x** |
| adaptive, pull-out 1.5, r 0.30 | 26 | 121.93 ms | 64.92 ms | **1.88x** |
| adaptive, push-in 1.5, r 0.15 / detail 4 | 51 | 41.02 ms | 21.69 ms | **1.89x** |

201 repeats on the fixed rows and 22 on the adaptive ones, macOS arm64, Release,
`cpu-only`. The fixed rows assert an equal applied count on both arms (10/10 and
25/25), so the two arms time the same work. The ADAPTIVE rows do not and cannot:
the carried arm applies one stamp more on each fixture (11/10, 26/25, 51/50),
because the re-gathering arm's last stamp had decayed to moving nothing. The
carried arm is therefore doing slightly MORE work for the smaller number.
An independent re-derivation against a separate build read 1.93x and 1.86x on
the fixed path and 1.53x / 1.88x / 1.88x on the adaptive one — so read the fixed
saving as 1.8–1.9x rather than a single figure. **Mac numbers; no device
measurement was taken for this change** and the mesh brushes are not in the
device suite.

**`mesh::MeshSculptor`, `mesh::MultiresSculptor` and `mesh::DynamicSculptor`
each gained `begin_carried_region()` / `end_carried_region()` / `carrying()`**,
and `DynamicSculptor` also `carried_counters()`, `carried_entries()`,
`carried_live()` and `carried_top_weight()`. A caller that drives `stamp`
directly is unaffected unless it opens one. `mesh::RemeshHooks` is new in
`remesh_local.h` and both `remesh_region` and `relax_region` take a defaulted
`const RemeshHooks*`; every existing call compiles and behaves unchanged.

**Two brushes an artist thinks of as one now disagree about reach.** The SDF
Move brush (`clay_sdf_move_*`, `layer.move_surface`, `Deformer::grab`) still
moves LESS than the displacement asked for, because its region weight is taken
at the sample point rather than at its preimage. That is a field-inversion
problem and not an anchoring one, and this change does not touch it.

### What it took on the adaptive surface

Stated because a host reading only the rule above will underestimate what the
adaptive path had to do. It retires vertex ids when it collapses and creates
them when it splits, BETWEEN the stamps of a gesture, so a carried region left
alone decays: 7 to 13 of 45 captured vertices survived an eleven-stamp stroke,
and at a brush radius of 0.15 against a detail resolution of 4 the remesher
retired ALL of them and the reach fell to 2.8–7.0% — BELOW the 9.3–22.3% that
re-gathering managed on the same fixtures.

Five rules, each measured:

1. The region is captured once and carried.
2. The remesh centre follows the stamp. Left at the anchor the tip is never
   reached: longest edge within a brush radius of the tip 0.1450 against 0.0487,
   25 vertices there against 65.
3. **A collapse may not retire a carried vertex** for the gesture's length.
   Without it, reach 72.98–97.10% and the two representations up to 0.375
   apart — worse than re-gathering. With it, 99.99–100.03% and 1.6e-4 on twelve
   fixtures. It leaves the surface FINER, not coarser: longest edge **0.2689**
   after a 1.5 push-in, against 0.6215 for the maintenance alone and 1.3150 with
   no remesher at all, on a sphere that starts uniform at 0.0917.
4. A split with one or both parents carried inserts its child from the parents'
   CAPTURED positions, never read off the surface.
5. A carried vertex the remesher moved takes the same shift in its captured
   position. Without it the relaxation inside a grab has no effect at all.

## A voxel falloff now means the curve it is named after

**`sculpt_grab` passed `BrushFalloff` where a `CEase` index was expected**
(#612, documented for hosts by #614; no ABI change, minor 0.117.0 line). The two
enums do not line up — `BrushFalloff` is Constant/Linear/Smooth/Gaussian = 0..3,
`CEase` is linear/smoothstep/smootherstep/in_quad = 0..3 — and `cregion_weight`
applies the ease to `(1 - d)`, so **every falloff delivered the NEXT one's
curve** and `Constant` was unreachable through the grab at all. Sampled at
d = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75:

| falloff | the name's curve | what the cast delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Constant | 1.000 1.000 1.000 1.000 | 1.000 0.750 0.500 0.250 |
| Linear | 1.000 0.750 0.500 0.250 | 1.000 0.844 0.500 0.156 |
| Smooth | 1.000 0.844 0.500 0.156 | 1.000 0.896 0.500 0.104 |
| Gaussian | 1.000 0.755 0.325 0.080 | 1.000 0.562 0.250 0.062 |

All four still ran 1 at the centre to 0 at the rim, so a grab still tapered and
nothing looked broken. What it cost was the MEANING of the control: one
`BrushParams` field meant two different things depending on which verb read it.
The grab now weights its inverse map with `falloff_weight`, the same table every
other voxel verb reads. Outside the ball is explicitly untouched, because
`falloff_weight` clamps rather than gates and `Constant` would otherwise weight
1 everywhere.

**This is HOST-VISIBLE and it is in "What moves under you" below.** A `Constant`
voxel grab now pulls rigidly inside the ball, where it used to taper linearly. A
host that wanted the old taper asks for `Linear`; a host that wanted the old
`Linear` asks for `Smooth`, and so on down the list. The curve values above are
the conversion table.

## A mask can now refuse a cell instead of dimming it

**`clay_brush_params` gained `mask_threshold`** (#611, ABI 0.117.0 — an APPEND
under `struct_size`). A mask gated a voxel edit by scaling the dither's weight,
`weight *= 1 - mask`, which is exact at the ends and probabilistic everywhere
between: a cell at mask 0.5 is written half the time, and a mask painted with a
falloff is mostly "in between", so a freeze leaked through its own skirt.

Measured on a slab with a six-cell mask skirt, one solid stroke: **25 cells at
mask ≥ 0.5 were erased anyway**, and masked work stood at 50.8% of unmasked
against a reporting host's bar of a quarter. (The proposal does not name the
machine for this measurement; the quantities it reports are counts and ratios
rather than times.)

At zero — the default, and what every existing caller passes — nothing changes,
bit for bit. Above zero the mask is read as a STENCIL: a cell at or above the
threshold is refused outright, and a cell below it is written **as though
unmasked**. That second half is not a detail: the threshold REPLACES the scaling
rather than putting a floor under it, because keeping the scaling below the
cutoff would leave exactly the dithered band a stencil exists to remove. A value
outside [0, 1] is REFUSED rather than clamped.

A host could not fix this from outside: `MaskField` exposes per-point `sample`
and `sample_many` and no bulk cell read, so binarising a mask per stroke meant
sampling every footprint cell from the host and rebuilding a second mask field
per dab.

## Relief is the SDF Inflate, and Standard is only approximated by it

**Measured, pinned by a test, and corrected everywhere a host reads it** (#615
and #618; **no ABI change, no kernel math change, no new op**). `docs/07` §9 and
the `CLAY_OP_RELIEF` comment both mapped ZBrush **Standard** and **Inflate** onto
one op. They cannot both be faithful: on a mesh the two differ in exactly one
column of the brush model, `clay_brush_frame` — Draw (the engine's own
`Standard` preset) displaces along ONE averaged normal per stamp, Inflate along
each vertex's own.

Relief offsets the accumulated field, and offsetting a distance moves each point
of the isosurface along the field's own gradient. That is the Inflate frame.
Four fixtures, each meshed at voxel 0.01 and stamped once at the standard clay
mapping (`k = rounding = region radius = 0.15`, reach 0.45). The table is each
frame-isolated reference's distance to the engine's relief surface, as a
fraction of `k`:

| fixture | normal spread under the stamp (mean / max) | inflate_ref: mean / max | draw_ref: mean / p95 / max |
|---|---|---|---|
| sphere, convex | 17.4 / 27.4 deg | 0.000 / 0.000 | 0.017 / 0.033 / 0.042 |
| torus inner equator, saddle | 36.6 / 74.3 deg | 0.000 / 0.000 | 0.077 / 0.204 / 0.356 |
| bowl, concave | 21.6 / 34.8 deg | 0.000 / 0.000 | 0.027 / 0.053 / 0.066 |
| thin fin, ridge | 79.2 / 95.2 deg | 0.001 / 0.093 | 0.568 / 1.091 / 1.278 |

**Relief IS Inflate**, to the precision of the measurement. How wrong it is as a
Standard depends on one thing: how far the normals under the stamp spread. On a
smooth form it is a few percent of the amplitude; on a feature narrower than the
stamp it is the whole amplitude:

| the fin after one stamp (`k = 0.15`) | top rise | half-thickness change at y = 0.4 |
|---|---|---|
| SDF Relief | +0.1500 | **+0.1500** (0.05 becomes 0.20) |
| mesh Inflate (engine) | +0.1500 | +0.1268 |
| mesh Draw (engine) | +0.1491 | **+0.0115** |

The fraction does not shrink with the amplitude — at `k = 0.05` the draw-frame
reference sits 0.024k / 0.102k / 0.039k / 0.598k from relief (sphere, saddle,
bowl, fin) — and when the stamp is wide against the form (region, rounding and
`k` all 0.3) it stops being small anywhere with curvature: 0.573k on the saddle
and 0.566k on the bowl.

**`CLAY_OP_INCISE` inherits the frame.** It is the same kernel branch with the
sign flipped, so everything above holds for it by construction. On a thin ridge
it severs rather than dents: one incise stamp at the standard mapping drops the
fin's top from 0.500 to 0.108 and leaves half-thickness 0 from y = 0.2 up.

What changed is the documentation and one test: `bindings/c/clay.h`,
`include/clay/kernel/tape.h`, `include/clay/scene/types.h`, `docs/07` §9,
`docs/09`'s inventory and coverage row, `docs/sculpt_comparison.md` and
`examples/25_relief.py`'s docstring all now name Relief as Inflate and Standard
as its approximation, with the condition that decides how far off.
`tests/unit/test_relief.cpp` pins the frame, so a future "fix" that turns Relief
into a Standard is seen to have changed Inflate.

**The faithful Standard was measured and is NOT shipped.** Three spellings were
tried. The exact draw frame is a warp of the accumulation, `a(p - k*w(p)*N)`; it
matches to 0.000k on all four fixtures and is not pointwise in `a`, which is
precisely what makes Relief cheap. Its first-order form needs the
accumulation's gradient, which the tape does not carry, and fails anyway where
it matters (0.129k mean / 0.733k p95 from the draw reference on the fin). The
engine can already spell it exactly as `Layer.move_surface(c, k*N, radius,
ease=smoothstep)`, but that is one per-item warp per dab: 30 dabs over a 24-item
blockout is **700 warps**, a 200k-point evaluation of **76.2 ms against 8.8 ms**
for the same stroke as relief, and a safe step scale of **1.5^-30 ≈ 5e-6**
against relief's 1/46. The proposal does not name the host for those times.

Hosts binding Standard to Relief keep working exactly as before. What changed is
that the divergence is written down rather than found.

## The adaptive surface gets undo, strokes, and recorded strokes

Three changes over four days, each with its own ABI minor.

### Undo and redo for an adaptive stroke, across the C ABI (#617, ABI 0.118.0)

A host holding a `clay_dynamic_surface` had no undo at all. `mesh::TopologyDelta`
records a stroke as one sparse reversible step and `session::History` replays
it, but nothing of it reached `clay.h`, and the adaptive surface lives outside
`clay_document` by design so the document's history cannot reach it either. The
only undo available was `clay_dynamic_surface_serialize` per stroke:
**3,195,040 bytes** on a 12,288-face sphere, against **1,233,636 bytes** for the
encoded record of the same stroke.

New: an opaque `clay_dynamic_delta` and nine entry points — `_create`,
`_destroy`, `_clear`, `_stats_get`, `_revert`, `_apply`, `_serialize`,
`_deserialize`, plus `clay_dynamic_sculptor_stamp_recorded`. `_stats_get`
reports per-kind entry counts, `resident_bytes`, and an **exact**
`encoded_bytes` of `56 + 122V + 114H + 42E + 66F`, held equal to what
`_serialize` writes by a C test and pinned by a `static_assert`.

Replay checks the surface's `{lineage, epoch}` mark against the record's end
mark and refuses a mismatch with `CLAY_ERROR_SNAPSHOT_MISMATCH` **before writing
anything**. It then erases, inserts and refits exactly the chunks the record
names and marks them dirty, so the sculptor's index stays current
**incrementally** — `rebuild_index` is neither needed nor appropriate (it clears
the dirty set, renumbers chunks, and costs O(surface)):

| faces | 30-stamp stroke | `revert` | incremental index | `rebuild_index` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 49,152 | 54.09 ms | 0.315 ms | 0.106 ms | 39.18 ms |
| 196,608 | 56.43 ms | 0.942 ms | 0.295 ms | 189.04 ms |
| 786,432 | 111.73 ms | 4.008 ms | 2.067 ms | **895.61 ms** |

All from `origin/main` at `aafeccb6`, cpu-only Release, **Apple arm64**, single
runs, illustrative only — nothing gates on them.

**It also FIXED a defect that was already shipping.** The relax pass in
`remesh_local.cpp` refreshed normals over incident faces and never noted or
re-synced them, so the record's `after` normals were stale: 346 vertex normals
and 669 face normals disagreed with the live surface immediately after capture.
Redo restored the wrong normals on every fixture tried, **up to 2,785 vertex
normals** on a 16-stamp stroke at detail 16; undo was wrong on two fixtures
(14 normals on one, 2,910 summed over eight strokes reversed then redone).
Positions and indices were exact in every row, which is why it went unseen:
`test_dynamic_history.cpp`'s comparison looked at positions and indices, and
`test_topology_delta.cpp` drives the operators without the relax pass. A host
read the wrong normals through `clay_dynamic_surface_copy_chunk` and
`clay_dynamic_surface_to_mesh`. It is fixed here with a regression test; with
`relax_after_remesh = false` there was never a discrepancy.

pyclay gains a `TopologyDelta` class and `DynamicSculptor.stamp(..., record=)`;
the Swift smoke gains a capture/undo/redo round-trip.

### A whole stroke reaches the adaptive surface (#619, ABI 0.119.0)

Spacing, the pressure curve, taper, jitter, steady stroke, accumulation, the
stylus azimuth and the brush presets reached the fixed mesh and the hierarchy
and not the adaptive surface: there was no `brush::apply_to_dynamic`, no C ABI
stroke call, no pyclay stroke method. There is now `brush::apply_to_dynamic`,
`clay_dynamic_sculptor_apply_stroke` and `_apply_preset`,
`DynamicSculptor.apply_stroke` / `.apply_preset`, and a Swift smoke case.

A host's own resolve-then-stamp loop is **not** an equivalent, and that is
measured rather than argued. On a `cube_sphere(24, 1.0)`, default
`DynamicTopologySettings` (detail 8), preset radius 0.3, reach as a share of the
drag:

| stroke | path | reach | stamps that moved a vertex |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snakehook, pull out 0.8, 14 stamps | dynamic, host loop | **42%** | 10/14, last stamp moved **0** |
| | dynamic, stroke rules | **96%** | 13/14 |
| | fixed, host loop | 50% | 11/14 |
| | fixed, `apply_to_mesh` | 98% | 13/14 |

Snakehook is lost by a host loop on both representations: the centre vertex
moves by its falloff weight rather than the whole delta, the brush falls behind
the cursor, and a few stamps later it is outside its own radius. The anchoring
rule lives in `brush` and a host cannot be expected to rediscover it.

**The adaptive path needed one rule the fixed path never did.** `apply_to_mesh`
anchors Snakehook on a weld class and a fixed mesh never loses one; the adaptive
surface remeshes before and after every Snakehook stamp and a collapse retires
vertex ids. An adaptive Snakehook therefore revalidates its anchor every stamp,
and re-finds it at the PREVIOUS STAMP position — never worse than re-finding at
the dead anchor's last position in any of the sixteen detail-4/8 rows measured
(equal in eight, better in eight, by up to 24 points: 57% → 81%). Keeping a dead
anchor costs 15–18% reach in the rows where the anchor died 6–14 times and
41–88% where it died 1–3 times.

**`MeshBrush::Layer` is REFUSED, not remapped** — `apply_to_dynamic` applies
nothing and returns 0, and both bindings refuse it with the reason
`dynamic_offers` records. `MeshStrokeOptions::defer_normals` is refused too: the
adaptive sculptor has no normal deferral, and accepting a flag and ignoring it
is exactly the failure the brush-engine spec forbids. Every stamp goes through
`DynamicSculptor::stamp`, so the **per-verb remesh schedule** (`default_timing`:
Grab AFTER, Clay BEFORE, Snakehook BEFORE+AFTER) runs on every stamp of a stroke
exactly as it does for a single one. `orient_alpha_by_stamp` is the same switch
the other two consumers take, and for the same reason: with it off, two strokes
differing only in azimuth are bit-identical.

**The call does not buy latency and does not claim to.** Draw at strength 0.4,
60 samples resolving to 14 stamps, radius 0.15, detail 6, `cube_sphere(48)`,
Release `libclay_shared`, fresh surface per run, arms alternated per iteration,
median of 30 after a warm-up: `clay_dynamic_sculptor_apply_stroke` 46.221 ms
against the resolve-then-stamp host loop's 46.048 ms — **1.004x**, identical
split counts (1107). The entry point is justified by the stroke's MEANING and by
the parity gate, not by speed. (An earlier 1.001x figure priced the host loop
before the call existed; 1.004x is the re-measurement and the one to use. The
proposal does not name the host for either.)

**Building the stroke exposed a defect in the single stamp.**
`DynamicSculptor::stamp_impl`'s reached-nothing exit returned the revisions it
read on ENTRY, so a Clay stamp whose BEFORE remesh changed the topology reported
that nothing had, and published the remesh counters twice. Neither exit carried
`hit_budget` (one also dropped `relaxed`), so a Grab whose remesh stopped at its
budget reported a converged region. A host re-uploading on a revision change was
the one who paid. Fixed with two regressions.

### A whole stroke as ONE guarded undo step (#622, ABI 0.120.0)

#617 and #619 landed within a day of each other and did not meet: the only
recorded path across the ABI was a host loop of `clay_stroke_resolve_full` plus
one `clay_dynamic_sculptor_stamp_recorded` per stamp. **That loop is a different
stroke, and its record is a faithful undo of the wrong surface.** On a
`cube_sphere(16)` through the C ABI, 24 samples, preset radius 0.3 / spacing 0.25
/ taper_end 0.3, strength 0.4, brush-relative detail 6:

| verb | stamps | host loop == the stroke (positions) | encoded bytes, host loop | encoded bytes, recorded stroke |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Draw | 6 | **yes** | 664,260 | 664,260 |
| Snakehook | 6 | **no** | 1,107,948 | 1,205,836 |
| Grab | 6 | **no** | 1,069,644 | 448,280 |

Draw is not a drag, so the loop and the stroke coincide down to the byte count.
Grab and Snakehook are drags: the stroke centres Grab on the first stamp and
Snakehook on the vertex it drags; the loop centres both on the cursor. So this
closed a **correctness** gap, not only an ergonomic one — a host had to choose
between the right stroke and an undo step.

New: `clay_dynamic_sculptor_apply_stroke_recorded` and
`_apply_preset_recorded` (the shipped calls' arguments plus a
`clay_dynamic_delta* record`), `brush::apply_to_dynamic_recorded`, and `record=`
on pyclay's `DynamicSculptor.apply_stroke` / `.apply_preset`. A NULL record
behaves exactly as the unrecorded call. The shipped
`clay_dynamic_sculptor_apply_stroke` / `_apply_preset` are unchanged.

The mark is checked **once**, before the first stamp, and a mismatch refuses the
whole stroke with nothing applied and the record untouched. That is not an
optimisation but a measured structural fact: across all seven fixtures (97
stamps) a per-stamp loop of `stamp_recorded` under the stroke's own rules
recorded **zero** refusals at k > 0, and injecting one unrecorded stamp before
k = 3 made the counter fire three times. A per-stamp check would be a check that
cannot fire. The mark check runs AFTER every `CLAY_ERROR_INVALID_ARGUMENT`
refusal, so a malformed call is never reported as retryable.

Undo and redo are exact on all seven fixtures — `to_mesh` positions, normals and
indices bit-identical, stored vertex and face normals and positions over every
live element bit-identical, `validate` ok, and no live face missing from the
sculptor's index after replay — including a 61-stamp Snakehook with 11 anchor
deaths. The recorded stroke's surface, applied count, split count and
moved-vertex count equal the unrecorded `apply_to_dynamic`'s.

**Recording costs what recording its stamps costs.** Draw, 14 stamps, topology
default, fresh surface per run, arms interleaved, Release, median:

| surface | runs | unrecorded stroke | recorded, fresh record | recorded, reused record | `stamp_recorded` loop |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `cube_sphere(48)`, 27,648 faces | 61 | 71.803 ms | 75.070 (**1.046x**) | 75.054 (1.045x) | 75.324 (1.049x) |
| `cube_sphere(96)`, 110,592 faces | 21 | 97.912 ms | 104.289 (**1.065x**) | 103.866 (1.061x) | 103.879 (1.061x) |

The band across runs is about 1.05–1.07x; a reused record saves under 1%. All
splits and encoded byte counts were equal across arms — that equality is the
assertion, and the clock is only the header's statement. Release, arm64; the
proposal does not name the host further.

## Faster

Four performance changes, each preserving exact output. **None of them closes
issue #531, and each says so in its own PR** — see Known limits.

**Shared source-grid samples in SDF initialization** (#613). Smooth/Relax
initialization evaluated identical halo positions in neighbouring bricks. Each
unique position is now evaluated once for sufficiently large, complete
uncomposed source grids and scattered into the existing brick layout; partial
and composed-prefix paths are untouched. For a 13×13×13 grid, evaluated samples
fall **1,601,613 → 1,157,625 (27.7%)** and requested scratch payload 3.6%. In a
retained engine probe, three alternating process pairs, first sample discarded,
pinned to one CPU: initial full materialization **214.076 → 156.149 ms (27.1%)**
at 32 source items and **10.685 → 8.964 ms (16.1%)** at one. All complete output
blobs match byte for byte. Host: **Intel Core i9-12900K, Linux, Release,
one logical CPU per performance core.** The other fixtures take fallback paths
and their small mixed differences are not attributed to this change.

**Batched normal refreshes in adaptive remeshing** (#616). Private topology
operators now refresh normals immediately for the first 64 successful updates
and then collect handles; after all topology passes the collector deduplicates
by slot AND generation, skips dead handles, refreshes each surviving normal
once, and flushes before relaxation. Twenty measured Draw stamps per process,
median of three per-process statistics, stamp/remesh milliseconds:

## What moves under you

### The formats

**Nothing.** `.clayspace` and the scene format do not move, `kSceneMinor` is
unchanged, and no record or document written by the previous release reads
differently. The kernel artifact's parity fixture is unchanged: **no kernel math
changed in this release.** The only edit under `include/clay/kernel/` is a
comment (#618, `tape.h`, naming Relief's frame).

`clay_dynamic_delta`'s serialized form is NEW in this release (ABI 0.118.0) and
is not a document format; it is keyed on the surface's element handles and on a
`{lineage, epoch}` mark, and is only replayable onto the surface it came from,
last in first out.

### The entry points

**A mesh `grab` reaches the whole drag** (#624). The largest behavioural break
in this release, on `clay_mesh_sculptor_apply_stroke`,
`clay_dynamic_sculptor_apply_stroke`, `clay_multires_sculptor_apply_stroke`,
their `_preset` and `_recorded` siblings, the three `brush::apply_to_*`
consumers and the five pyclay methods. **No version gate announces it**, because
no symbol was added, no descriptor re-laid out, and no ABI number moved for it.
Every preset changes by a different amount — see the table at the top.

**A voxel `grab`'s falloff produces a different curve** (#612). `Constant` now
pulls rigidly where it used to taper linearly; `Linear` gives Linear where it
used to give Smooth; `Smooth` gives Smooth where it used to give Gaussian-ish;
`Gaussian` gives Gaussian where it used to give a sharper curve. No symbol
changed and no field moved. A host that liked what it had should shift its
request one step down the list.

**`clay_brush_params` grew `mask_threshold`** (#611, ABI 0.117.0). An APPEND
under `struct_size`: a caller compiled against the older struct passes the older
size and gets today's dimmer behaviour, bit for bit. A caller that sets it above
zero gets a stencil, in which cells below the threshold are written **fully**,
not partially.

**`CLAY_OP_RELIEF` and `CLAY_OP_INCISE` mean what they always meant** (#618) —
the enum values are unchanged at 14 and 15 and no arithmetic moved. What changed
is the documentation: Relief is the SDF **Inflate**, and binding ZBrush
**Standard** to it is an approximation that thickens features narrower than the
stamp by up to the whole amplitude. A host binding Standard to Relief keeps the
output it had; it now knows what it is.

**A `clay_dynamic_sculptor_stamp` report is accurate on a reached-nothing stamp**
(#619). The stale-revision and doubled-counter exits are fixed, and both exits
now carry `hit_budget` and `relaxed`. A host re-uploading on a revision change,
or branching on `hit_budget`, sees different values than it used to on stamps
that reached nothing or whose remesh hit its budget.

**Adaptive redo restores the right normals** (#617). Before this release, redo
through `mesh::TopologyDelta` could restore up to 2,785 wrong vertex normals on
a single stroke (undo could too, on some fixtures). Any host that built its own
undo on the C++ `TopologyDelta` was reading those normals through
`clay_dynamic_surface_copy_chunk` and `_to_mesh`.

## Known limits, stated rather than discovered

### From this release

- **No device measurement for the mesh grab change (#624).** Every number for it
  is macOS arm64, Release, `cpu-only`. The reference iPad gate was not run for
  it and the mesh brushes are not in the device suite.
- **A fixed mesh has no mitigation for a stretched captured region**, and cannot
  have one: the same weld classes are carried however far the gesture goes, so a
  1.5 pull leaves a longest edge of **0.8176** from a starting **0.1174**. That
  is what a fixed-topology move brush is. Only the adaptive path grows its
  region.
- **On a CURVE the adaptive surface reaches PAST the chord** — **115.79%**
  against the fixed path's 100.00% on a quarter arc, a disagreement of 0.2221 —
  because the gesture adopts splits born on material the arc swept and then
  carries them. The two representations agree to 1e-3 on that fixture only with
  the remesher off, so the 1.6e-4 agreement above is a straight-drag number.
  Measured, not fixed, and not a regression against re-gathering, which
  disagreed by 1.3e-3 on the same fixture.
- **The carried region has no upper bound.** On the adaptive path it grows with
  the splits — 45 entries becoming **811** on a 1.5 pull-out. A very long gesture
  over an already-dense region has not been measured.
- **The collapse protection has been measured only on grab strokes on a
  sphere**, twelve fixtures of 11 to 51 stamps. A gesture whose carried region
  covers a region the artist then wants thinned has not been.
- **There is no faithful SDF Standard, and building one needs its own
  proposal** (#618). The draw-frame op cannot be a combine op (it needs the
  accumulated field at another point), its first-order form needs a gradient the
  tape does not carry, and the exact spelling that exists —
  `Layer.move_surface` with a smoothstep ease — costs 700 warps and 76.2 ms
  against relief's 8.8 ms over a 30-dab stroke on a 24-item blockout, with a safe
  step scale of 5e-6 against 1/46. Nothing here is an engine change.
- **ClayBuildup is unmeasured, not approximated by a stated amount** (#618). Its
  SDF mapping is Relief along a stroke with buildup accumulation; the mesh `Clay`
  clamps Draw's deposit to a plane, a third behaviour. No number in this release
  is about it.
- **Relief's stepping bound is already poor at stroke density** (#618): 30
  overlapping dabs declare L = 46 on the whole-document tape. It is additive and
  per record, not overlap-aware.
- **Adaptive undo is LAST-IN FIRST-OUT only, and nothing compacts** (#617).
  Spatially disjoint strokes are not independent — a later stroke reused 27
  vertex, 161 half-edge, 80 edge and 54 face slots a stroke on the opposite
  hemisphere had freed, and reverting the earlier one under it failed `validate`.
  The mark refuses any replay that is not LIFO. Dead slots stay after an undo:
  face slots went 12,288 → 14,964 and surface `bytes` 3,637,376 → 6,684,928 after
  three undos, reported as `clay_dynamic_surface_stats.dead_slots`. Compaction
  would renumber the handles every held record is keyed on.
- **`serialize` is NOT the test for "undo restored the surface"** (#617). The
  serialized size was unchanged (3,195,040) but the bytes were not identical,
  because the slot pools retired differently. The exact comparison is `to_mesh`
  plus `validate`.
- **A second sculptor over the same surface is not kept in step** (#617). Not
  new — a stamp through one sculptor already leaves the other's index stale —
  but now stated in the header beside the replay calls.
- **The C++ `session::History` path is unchanged** (#617). It resolves a
  `DynamicSurface` rather than a sculptor, so a C++ caller who keeps a sculptor
  across `History::undo` still gets a stale index. No ABI caller can reach that
  path.
- **`bytes()` is a budget, not an assertion** (#617). It returned 1,884,384
  against a 1,261,256-byte payload, because it counts capacities and slot maps.
  `encoded_bytes` is the exact figure.
- **A recorded stroke ACCUMULATES into its record** (#622). One `RecordedGesture`
  taking a dab then a Clay stroke then a Grab stroke grew 707,358 → 1,263,002 →
  1,647,616 encoded bytes, and ONE revert restored the pristine surface. That is
  allowed and deliberate — so **one undo step per stroke means clearing the
  record, or making a new one, before each stroke.** The engine does not do it
  for you.
- **The adaptive stroke calls are not in the device coverage gate** (#622).
  `check_device_coverage.py` matches `clay_dynamic_sculptor_stamp(` exactly, and
  neither `_stamp_recorded` nor `_apply_stroke` is in its verb list. This release
  records that rather than widening the gate.
- **`#531` is NOT closed and the all-brush 16 ms target is UNMET.** Every one of
  #613, #616, #621 and #623 says so in its own PR, and this release repeats it
  rather than letting four improvements imply otherwise. #616's slowest
  individual candidate stamps are 24.899 ms (footprint 1,000) and 71.785 ms
  (10,000) with undo; #623's application results are mixed outside Smooth/Relax;
  #613's engine probes "alone do not establish the 16 ms application target";
  #621 makes no timing claim at all.
- **#621 has no uncontended timing.** Its only measured claim is an allocation
  count on one fixture (126 → 80). Its paired run was under CPU contention and
  is correctness evidence only.
- **Native GPU execution of the #613/#616/#621/#623 changes was not measured.**
  #623's desktop comparison reports CUDA but is not a CPU-only run and does not
  isolate the GPU. The Metal parity leg says the backends agree, not that the
  device is faster.
- **`p95` in #616's table is a small-sample statistic** (three per-process
  medians of twenty stamps), not a population tail estimate.
- **Four proposals do not name the host they measured on** — #611, #615/#618,
  #619 and #622. Where a machine is stated (#617 Apple arm64; #613/#616/#621/#623
  Intel i9-12900K Linux; #624 macOS arm64) this document repeats it; where it is
  not, this document says so rather than inventing one.

### Unchanged from v0.116.0

- **Decimation can still emit a non-manifold mesh at some ratios on some
  toolchains** (#575, closed as attributed rather than fixed). Per-file impact is
  one or two edges in 126k–210k triangles, watertight throughout. The root cause
  is upstream of decimation: `-ffp-contract=on` and `off` differ by 24 triangles
  on a unit sphere at voxel 0.02, with 31 of 76 ratios pinching on one and 0 of
  76 on the other (#581). `clay_mesh_decimate_report` is how a host learns its
  export is affected.
- **A mesh is not byte-identical across toolchains.** Anything pinning a triangle
  count, a vertex position or a mesh digest across compilers rests on something
  that moves.
- **Regional consolidation's local path is narrow by construction.** It requires
  an isolated visible hard-Add volume, identity node and layer placement, unit
  scale axes, no rounding, gates or active replication, grab-only deformers with
  positive radii and exactly zero easing outside their support, and no other
  visible operand reaching the patch halo. Everything else takes the conservative
  whole-root path.
- **`clay_stroke_preset`'s `rotate_to_azimuth` and `velocity_*` controls read
  channels only `clay_stroke_sample_full` carries.** On the `count*5` float
  packing the other six stroke entry points take, a barrel stamp faces a constant
  +x and the speed response is off. `clay_stroke_resolve_full` into
  `clay_layer_place_stamps` is the path that carries them — and, as of #619,
  `clay_dynamic_sculptor_apply_stroke`, which takes `clay_stroke_sample_full`
  too. Unchanged from v0.116.0, which carried it from v0.113.0.
- **The flat-packed mesh and multires stroke calls still take `count*5` floats**
  and so cannot carry an azimuth. #619 did not widen them.
- **Move still builds no stroke preset**, so spacing, pressure and lazy-mouse
  have nowhere to land on it beyond `steady` (#532). Unchanged from v0.116.0,
  which carried it from v0.113.0.
- **The SDF Move brush still moves less than the displacement asked for**,
  because its region weight is taken at the sample point rather than at its
  preimage. #624 did not touch it, and the two brushes an artist thinks of as one
  now disagree about reach.

### Unchanged, and carried from `docs/RELEASE.md`'s open items

- **"`clay_list_backends` reports metal on a real iPad" has not been observed by
  this repository** (issue #45). The release workflow builds the xcframework on
  macOS and fails rather than shipping a CPU-only slice, and the Swift smoke
  asserts the backend registers — but the verification is on the build, not on
  hardware.
- **`check_gallery.py` gates a manifest, not an image.** A marched mesh's vertex
  count is not a constant of the document — three of the gallery meshes disagree
  between an M-series Mac and an ubuntu-latest runner — so the gate cannot assert
  byte or element equality, and an output that merely changed still needs a full
  `examples/run_all.py` regeneration to be caught.

## Upgrading

Ordered by how much risk each item carries.

1. **Re-tune any host feel calibrated against a mesh grab's reach** (#624). The
   table at the top is the per-preset factor; there is no single one and no
   opt-out is offered. A policy flag here would mean shipping both a brush that
   follows the cursor and one that does not, and asking the host to choose. This
   is the one change most likely to alter what an artist sees with no warning at
   all: no symbol moved and no ABI number announces it.
2. **Calibrate a curved drag against `last_sample − first_sample`, not the path
   length** (#624). The surface moves the chord.
3. **Re-check every voxel grab that names a falloff** (#612). Shift your request
   one step down the list to keep the curve you had: what you got as `Constant`
   is now `Linear`, what you got as `Linear` is now `Smooth`, what you got as
   `Smooth` is roughly `Gaussian`. `Constant` is now reachable and rigid.
4. **If you built your own adaptive undo on `mesh::TopologyDelta`, re-record it**
   (#617). Records captured before this release carry stale normals, and a
   `RecordedGesture` is what the ABI replays; a raw `TopologyDelta` has no marks
   and cannot go through `DynamicSculptor::replay`.
5. **If you branch on a `clay_dynamic_stamp_report` from a stamp that reached
   nothing, or on `hit_budget`, re-read that branch** (#619). Both were wrong
   before and are right now.
6. **If you record an adaptive stroke, clear or replace the record between
   strokes** (#622). The record accumulates by design, and one undo step per
   stroke is the host's choice to make, not the engine's.
7. **Re-record any golden that drives a grab through a stroke consumer** (#624).
   Two cases in this repository moved and are the only two:
   `test_dynamic_stroke.cpp`'s "a stroke equals its stamps" and "a recorded
   stroke is the unrecorded stroke". `mesh_sculpt_goldens_*.inc` are NOT
   affected — the parity case drives `MeshSculptor::stamp` with an explicit
   centre and never a stroke consumer.
8. **If you bind ZBrush Standard to `CLAY_OP_RELIEF`, nothing changes and
   everything is now written down** (#618). Bind **Inflate** to it faithfully;
   keep Standard bound to it only if your forms are smooth at the brush's scale.
   For a single exact draw-frame stamp use `clay_layer_move_surface` with a
   smoothstep ease — not for a stroke of them.
9. **`clay_brush_params` gained one field** (#611). Recompile and pass
   `sizeof(clay_brush_params)` as `struct_size` to reach `mask_threshold`, or
   keep the old size and keep the old dimmer behaviour exactly. If you set it,
   remember that cells below it are written **fully** rather than partially, and
   that a value outside [0, 1] is refused rather than clamped.
10. **Nothing to do for any format, for the kernels artifact, or for any other
    verb.** No document, scene or kernel fixture changed, and no existing call
    signature changed.
