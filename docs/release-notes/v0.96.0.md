# claycore v0.96.0

Twelve minor versions in one tag: 0.85.0 through 0.96.0, cut two days after
v0.84.0. The theme of v0.84.0 was that **the engine stops paying for what it
provably cannot reach**. This one points the other way: **the engine already
knew; a host could not ask.**

Most of what follows adds no capability to the engine. A group could always
name a region — nothing could turn that name into a selection. Three automask
factors were computed and applied — none of the lattices two of them read could
be handed across the C ABI. A stroke's points were always in *some* space — the
header never said which, and two callers of one estimator quietly picked
different ones. A document could always be written — never at the format
version last month's build can open. In each case the missing piece was a
sentence, a struct, or an entry point, not an algorithm.

The exceptions are real and they are the layer work: an SDF layer gains a
**boolean operator**, so `A − B + C` is a document rather than a bake; a
multires hierarchy **survives the file**; and an intersecting drag stops
re-meshing the layer it drags through.

**26 merged PRs, 222 commits on main. The C ABI gained 40 entry points with
nothing removed and no existing signature changed** — 639 declarations at
v0.84.0, all 639 still present, 679 now. The `.clayspace` and scene formats both
go **17 → 19**.

Three issues close: **#471** (an intersect drag re-meshes the whole layer),
**#472** (a mesh layer's revision does not move when history replaces its
triangles) and **#495** (a placed layer's cavity automask and its painted cavity
mask disagree about the same crevice).

## A layer composes (ABI 0.87.0, #477)

`scene::Layer` carried a name, a transform, protection and visibility, and no
operator. Visible SDF layers hard-unioned, unconditionally, and the compiler
said so in eight places — a layer was organisation and nothing else. An artist
who wanted one shape to cut another had to put both in one layer, and lose the
ability to hide, reorder or transform the cutter as a thing.

An SDF layer now carries a `LayerComposition` — op, blend, `blend_k`, rounding —
built from the **existing item-level enums**, so there is one vocabulary rather
than two and no second evaluator. `Compiler::emit_layer_fold` is the single seam
rule, called by both `run` and `resume`.

Three details decided the design rather than following from it:

- **The first visible SDF layer initialises the accumulator and its own operator
  is not applied.** `Subtract(empty, A)` and `Intersect(empty, A)` are the two
  ways a stack would otherwise open with nothing on screen and no error.
  First-ness is a **type** (`FirstVisibleLayer`), not a second bool, so
  transposing it is a compile error rather than a blank viewport.
- **An absent operand is still an operand.** `layer_val` is false both for an
  empty layer and for one wholly culled in this region; the fold emits an
  explicit empty and combines whenever the operator reads absence as a change —
  decided by probing `ctape_combine_dist` rather than by tabulating which ops
  those are.
- Bounds are widened by the fold's own support, and exactness and Lipschitz fold
  exactly as the item combine folds them.

Hiding a subtractive layer restores the uncut geometry exactly. Reordering is a
modelling decision. The consuming host ranked this the only open row that
changed what it could ship: a subtool *is* a layer there, so an item-level
cutter never reached the workflow, and what it shipped instead was a resolved
boolean that stopped tracking its operands the moment one moved.

## Three placements, and advice about what a bake costs (ABI 0.86.0, #476)

`clay_layer_snap_to_ground`, `clay_layer_zero_to_origin` and
`clay_layer_centre_bounds` are the three placements a host was writing by hand
out of `clay_layer_set_transform` and a bounds query, and
`clay_layer_consolidation_advice` answers the question that precedes a bake:
the resolution to bake at, and what it will cost. `clay_layer_node_color` and
`clay_layer_set_transform_bound` (ABI 0.89.0) close the same kind of gap — a
value the engine held with no route to a caller.

## Regional multires, finished and persisted (ABI 0.88.0–0.89.0, 0.92.0; #469, #481, #478, #485)

Depth becomes a property of a **base patch** rather than of the surface: level 5
on the nose, and the boots left alone. Catmull-Clark multiplies faces by four a
level, so three levels past what the boots need is 64x the geometry nobody will
sculpt.

**The audit came first and it moved the design.** On a 1,600-patch cage, an
unsculpted level costs **0.0 MB of detail** — `DetailField` is already sparse
and it was never the prize. What a level costs is topology, the evaluated
buffers and the chunk index, every byte proportional to the FACE count. A change
that had made the coefficients sparser would have delivered nothing.

What #481 finished:

- **A cross-level neighbourhood.** A level's vertices reach their neighbours
  across a depth boundary, over a joined numbering in the dense level's order. A
  coarse neighbour has no face at this level to name, so the faces the dense
  level *would* have had are derived rather than looked up.
- **Mixed-depth export.** `mixed_mesh_at_level` emits one welded mesh at **0
  open edges**, against 72/168/264 for the per-patch loop it replaces. The quad
  list survives only where no edge is split — a split edge makes a coarse quad a
  pentagon, which has no quadrangulation.
- **Crossing stamps.** A stamp anchored near a boundary writes the coarse side
  too, restoring what the level above owns.

#485 then built the **boundary frame** on that neighbourhood, which #481 had
deliberately left unticked rather than promised. The frame needs a raw Newell
sum; `normal_contribution` on the neighbourhood is angle-weighted and is the
wrong quantity for a frame, which is the kind of distinction that is invisible
until a rim twists.

#478 makes a hierarchy **survive the file**: an `'MRES'` chunk keyed by layer id
— an identity that outlives a process, which a handle address does not. Before
it, the engine reported a hierarchy's row as an ordinary MESH layer, so the
host's side-car file was *the only thing in the world that knew the row had ever
been a hierarchy*. Lose it — a copy, a move between machines, an export to
anyone without their application — and the sculptor's levels were gone and the
row came back as the flat cage. The host had made that loss loud in three panels
and a diagnostics report, and recorded that **loud is not fixed**.

The home was **forced, not chosen**: `check_layering.py` withholds `clay/mesh`
from `clay::scene`, so a hierarchy physically cannot be owned by a
`scene::Layer`. It lives beside the document keyed by layer id, exactly as voxel
grids, masks and imported meshes do.

## A group is a selection (ABI 0.85.0, #467)

Surface groups name a region. Masks gate what a verb may touch. The two
interoperated in **one** direction only — `clay_groups_fill_from_mask` took a
painted mask and named it — so a group could reach a brush only through
`CLAY_AUTOMASK_SURFACE_GROUP`, *"stay inside the polygroup the brush started
in"*, and never through a mask a caller **chose**.

That is a different operation from the one an artist performs. Naming a region
and deciding what to do to it are two acts, in that order. "Flatten this whole
panel" was expressible only as a stroke that happened to begin on it, and every
verb that already respects a mask — freeze, invert-and-sculpt-the-rest, relax,
extrude — was unreachable from a group for the same reason.
`clay_mask_fill_from_group` is the other direction.

## Say which space you mean (ABI 0.93.0; #486, #496, #475 — issue #495)

A painted cavity mask and a cavity **automask** protected different crevices of
the same surface, on any layer that had been moved. `mesh/automask.h` states the
requirement it was breaking:

> The requirement is that a painted cavity mask and a cavity automask cannot
> disagree about one surface, and the way to guarantee that is to have exactly
> one estimator.

The estimator *was* one. The **question** was two. Both the cavity field and the
group lattice are world-addressed and a sculptor's vertices are the mesh's own,
so a vertex has to be placed before either is asked. `mesh_mask_gate` placed it.
`mesh_automask_inputs`, three lines below it in the same anonymous namespace,
did not — it sampled at the raw mesh-space point. **One estimator does not
prevent a disagreement when its two callers ask it about different points.**

#486 is the general form: a mesh sculpting session now declares which space it
speaks (`clay_mesh_sculptor_set_world_frame`,
`clay_mesh_sculptor_use_layer_transform`), and both the brush settings and the
stroke are converted into it rather than only the stamp. #475 wrote down which
space a stroke's points and a dirty region are in, which is the documentation
half of the same defect.

## An automask a host can reach (ABI 0.96.0, #494)

Five automask factors existed. Three — normal angle, topology connectivity,
boundary — crossed the C ABI as scalars and worked. Two did not: **cavity** and
**surface group** need world-addressed lattice inputs, and there was no way to
hand a host's mask or group field to a sculptor. Setting the bit did nothing,
silently: the factor was computed against an absent input and lifted nothing.

`clay_automask_sources` carries the cavity mask, the group field and the active
group, with the three setters that take one —
`clay_mesh_sculptor_set_automask_sources` and its dynamic and multires
counterparts. Measured on the fixture: the bit alone and no sources lift
14.6555, identical to ungated; the sources alone and no bit, identical again;
both, 0. Strength interpolates (0.25 → 10.9916, 0.50 → 7.32773) and the two
groups partition the lift exactly (6.248 + 8.40746 = 14.6555).

## Where a dab's time went (ABI 0.94.0, #487)

A stage report a host can switch on per sculptor and read back
(`clay_mesh_sculptor_set_stage_report_enabled`, `..._stage_report`,
`..._reset_stage_report`, and the dynamic trio). It reports what each stage of a
dab cost **and what it did** — a duration alone cannot distinguish a stage that
was slow from one that was handed more work, which is the distinction a host
tuning a brush actually needs.

## One adjacency per mesh layer (ABI 0.91.0, #484)

Two sculptors over the same mesh layer each built and kept their own adjacency.
The cache now lives beside the triangles on the document, keyed by a
`TopologyFingerprint` (vertex count, triangle count, connectivity hash, weld
epsilon), with `clay_document_topology_cache_stats` and
`clay_document_trim_topology_cache` to see and bound it.

**It forgets at both chokepoints, and the second one is the whole change.**
`install_mesh_geometry` is where triangles enter; `note_mesh_geometry_replaced`
is where a weld rewrites them in place. Forgetting only at the first is not a
correctness bug — the fingerprint absorbs it — it is a *performance* bug that
looks like nothing: the weld path paid 120 ms where the cache promised 0.25 ms,
and every test still passed.

## A revision that never repeats (#488, #483 — issue #472)

`clay_document_mesh_layer_revision` is documented as bumped "every time a
layer's triangles are replaced wholesale", and the reason given for its
existence is the cache a wholesale replacement invalidates. **Undoing a rebuild
is exactly that change, and the revision did not move.** So the one moment the
number was added for was the one moment it was silent: a sculptor who rebuilds,
dislikes the result and undoes keeps working through an adjacency and a BVH
built over triangles that are no longer in the document, and it surfaces as a
refused stroke with nothing naming the cause. Eight writers touch the triangles;
three bumped.

The design note (#483) generalises it, and is the more useful half:

> Decide whether a change token identifies **content** or **an epoch of
> mutation**. If a consumer uses it to decide whether its derived state is
> stale, it is an epoch, it must be monotonic, and it must advance on every
> restoration — including the ones that put back bytes it has seen before.

Monotonic costs a rebuild after undo-then-redo: loud, bounded, correct.
Restoring the pre-undo value costs a silent wrong answer, unbounded.

## An intersect drag stops re-meshing the layer (ABI 0.90.0, #490 — issue #471)

An intersecting boolean drag re-meshed the whole layer every frame: **2.54x its
subtracting control** on the identical fixture, and **seconds per frame** at ten
times the extent.

What this is **not**: the layer-wide influence bound is untouched and correct.
`max(acc, item)` can change the field anywhere the layer has material, and an
arbitrary edit to an Intersect really can reach the whole layer. Weakening that
is the failure mode the engineering guide exists to prevent.

What it is: a second, narrower bound *beside* the influence bound, answering a
different question — *given this exact before node and after node, where can the
zero set relevant to refill and remeshing have changed?* That can be local even
when the field change is not. `item_geometry_reach_in_document` dilates the
item's geometry bound by its layer's `cull_pad`, by each enclosing group's
`group_blend_support`, and carries it up over every instancing layer;
`command_surface_delta_bound` returns it for `SetTransformCmd` on a visible
Intersect item with finite support, and `std::nullopt` for every other command,
every other op, and every case outside the proof. **Every support helper is
reused and none re-derived**, because two spellings of "how far a combine
reaches" will diverge.

## A document that saves at an older minor (ABI 0.95.0, #491)

`clay_document_save_at_minor`, `clay_document_save_memory_at_minor` and
`clay_document_writable_at_minor`: write at a named format layout, or **refuse
and say which layer blocks it** (`multires_blocking_minor`,
`document_blocking_minor`). The refusal is the feature. A save that silently
dropped the hierarchy a reader at minor 17 cannot represent is the same class of
loss #478 exists to prevent, one version later.

## The gate measures a session, not only a size (#492)

The device gate grew a **seventh session**: a sustained sculpt measured over its
*length* rather than its size, last and on its own, because a warm dab drifting
over thousands of dabs is what a session actually is and a cold burst cannot
show it. The ordering is thermal and measured, not chosen — the latency cases
read 1.00–1.15x of baseline running first and **1.34–2.16x running second**,
which fails six cases with nothing wrong with the engine.

`#493` records what the cross-level rim refresh costs and is deliberately left
open: 7.5–18.6% of a dab depending on rim length, scaling with the rim rather
than the region.

## What moves under you

### The formats

- **`.clayspace` and scene formats 17 → 19**, in two steps: **minor 18** at ABI
  0.87.0 with the layer composition, **minor 19** at ABI 0.88.0 with the
  persisted multires hierarchy. A document written by this build is not readable
  by a v0.84.0 build. Use `clay_document_save_at_minor` to write one that is —
  it refuses rather than silently dropping what an older layout cannot hold, and
  names the blocking layer.

### The entry points

- **`clay_multires_stamp_report.moved_vertices` changed meaning** (0.88.0): it
  is now summed over every level a stamp wrote, where it previously reported the
  bound level alone. The layout is unchanged, so this does not break a build —
  it changes an answer. Reporting the bound level alone made a stamp that moved
  only the coarse side come back `0`.
- **`clay_document_mesh_layer_revision` now advances on undo and redo.** If you
  cached against it on the assumption that it identifies content, it does not
  and never did — it identifies an epoch. A rebuild after undo-then-redo is the
  intended cost.
- **Visible SDF layers no longer hard-union unconditionally.** A document that
  relied on the implicit union is unchanged — union with default blend is the
  default composition — but a layer's operator is now a thing that can be set,
  and the first visible layer's operator is deliberately not applied.
- **Setting a cavity or surface-group automask bit without supplying sources
  still lifts nothing**, exactly as before. That is now fixable rather than
  silent: supply `clay_automask_sources`.

## Fixes

- **#471** — an intersect drag re-meshed the whole layer (2.54x its subtract
  control; seconds per frame at 10x extent).
- **#472** — a mesh layer's revision did not move when history replaced its
  triangles.
- **#495** — a placed layer's cavity automask sampled mesh space while its
  painted cavity mask sampled world space, so the two protected different
  crevices of one surface.
- A weld rewriting triangles in place did not invalidate the shared topology
  cache (#484). Not a wrong answer — the fingerprint caught it — but 120 ms
  where 0.25 ms was promised, with every test passing.
- Two `clay_multires` handles were leaked by a layer-composition fixture (520
  bytes, 5 allocations). macOS ASan cannot detect leaks at all
  (`detect_leaks is not supported on this platform`), so the local run was
  silently blind; `leaks --atExit` is the local substitute.

## Known limits, stated rather than discovered

- **The device gate has not produced a valid record for this range**, and the
  reference-iPad section above is empty rather than estimated. The 2026-09-07
  attempt died at session 3 of 7 to a thermal kill: RunningBoard force-terminated
  the host holding the `Developer testing` assertion as the device crossed into
  thermal `Warn`, with no crash report and no JetsamEvent, which is that kill's
  signature. 17 latency cases passed first. This is heat, not a code regression.
- **#497** — `sdf_stroke_in_group_bricks` reads 2.41x its device baseline,
  unattributed, and the baseline is 20 engine files stale. This is the case the
  thermal kill above landed on, which is a coincidence of ordering rather than
  evidence about the case. A stale baseline has two failure modes and this is
  the dangerous one: recent enough to compare, so it produces a red gate that
  looks like a finding, while the natural next move — re-baselining — records
  whatever the box did once and destroys the evidence.
- **#493** — the cross-level rim refresh is 7.5–18.6% of a dab and scales with
  the rim rather than the region. Open, measured, not fixed.
- **#243** — the Apple-blocked work (a Simulator parity run, and the QoS half of
  thread scheduling) is unchanged from v0.84.0.
- **A case below 0.125 ms cannot gate**, however stable it is: the check
  requires the absolute difference to clear a 0.05 ms noise floor as well as the
  tolerance. A perf win can push its own case under that floor and switch off
  the gate protecting it. Unchanged from v0.84.0.
- **The binding-parity gate reads pyclay's surface out of the nanobind
  registrations when no module is built**, printing `parsed` where an
  authoritative run prints `imported`. That is deliberate in the `checks` job,
  and `bindings/python/tests/test_binding_parity.py` runs the same gate with
  `--require-import` against the built module. Both readings must agree; read
  the line rather than the colour.

## Upgrading

Nothing removed, no signature changed, no struct re-laid out: 639 declarations
at v0.84.0, all 639 still present. A build against v0.84.0 headers compiles and
links against this one.

Three things to do deliberately, in rising order of risk:

1. **If you cache against `clay_document_mesh_layer_revision`**, re-read the
   contract: it is an epoch, not content, and it advances on redo. The cost of
   getting this wrong is a stroke landing against a mesh that no longer exists.
2. **If you set `CLAY_AUTOMASK_CAVITY` or `CLAY_AUTOMASK_SURFACE_GROUP`**,
   supply `clay_automask_sources` — the bit alone has never done anything and
   still does not.
3. **If you write documents other tools read**, decide a floor and call
   `clay_document_writable_at_minor` before saving, or
   `clay_document_save_at_minor` and handle the refusal. The default path writes
   minor 19, which a v0.84.0 reader cannot open.

### A note on the version numbers in this range

On main's first-parent line the ABI goes **0.90.0 → 0.96.0 in a single step**.
Seven pull requests (#484, #485, #486, #487, #492, #491, #494) were a linear
stack, each an ancestor of the next, and they landed as one merge that closed
all seven. So 0.91.0 through 0.95.0 exist as commit states inside that merge but
never as tags, and **no release was cut at any of them**. If you are looking for
a v0.95.0 tag, that is why there is not one.

Two related artefacts of the same collapse, since both would otherwise be
reconstructed wrongly: the range holds **21 commits rather than the 22** the
seven PRs produced, because the rebase onto 0.90.0 flattened #494's merge commit
— verified by name, since a count cannot tell a flattened merge from a dropped
one. And **#494 shows as merged into #491's branch** rather than into main,
because it was stacked on it.
